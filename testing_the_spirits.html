The Promised One
A Quiet Study · For a Friend

~ Discernment & Caution ~ Testing the Spirits

A reference companion — what the Bible itself says about how to weigh a prophet, a teaching, or a spiritual experience.

One of the kindest things the Bible does is warn us. Not warn us about strangers — warn us about our own susceptibility. About how easily a sincere heart can be led astray by something that looks right.

What follows is simply a list of the tests Scripture itself gives, with the verses laid out so you can read them. The Bible takes deception seriously. It assumes that compelling teachers, miraculous signs, and beautiful experiences can come from sources that aren't God — and it gives us the tools to tell the difference. Read the criteria, then apply them yourself, to anyone, including the people you already trust.

I · The Warning

The Bible expects deception

Jesus and the apostles did not speak of false prophets as a rare exception. They spoke of them as a near-certainty — frequent, persuasive, and dangerous precisely because of how convincing they would seem.

Matthew 24:24 — Jesus' own words
For false messiahs and false prophets will appear and perform great signs and wonders to deceive, if possible, even the elect.
Matthew 7:15
Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves.
1 John 4:1
Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world.
2 Peter 2:1
There will be false teachers among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them.
Acts 20:29–30 — Paul to the Ephesian elders
I know that after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock; and from among your own selves will arise men speaking twisted things.
II · The Tests

The criteria Scripture gives

The Bible doesn't ask anyone to take its claims on blind trust, and it doesn't ask anyone to take a prophet's claims on blind trust either. It gives concrete tests. Most of them are old — older than the New Testament, older than the temple. They were given by Moses to ancient Israel, sharpened by the prophets, and confirmed by Jesus and the apostles.

i.
A miracle is not, by itself, proof

This is perhaps the most counterintuitive teaching in the Bible — and the most important. Signs and wonders can be performed by sources other than God. The presence of a miracle does not authenticate the messenger.

Deuteronomy 13:1–3
If a prophet arises among you... and gives you a sign or a wonder, and the sign or wonder that he tells you comes to pass, and if he says, 'Let us go after other gods,' you shall not listen. For the Lord your God is testing you.
Exodus 7:11–12 — Pharaoh's magicians
Pharaoh summoned the wise men and the sorcerers, and they, the magicians of Egypt, also did the same by their secret arts. For each man cast down his staff, and they became serpents.
2 Thessalonians 2:9
The coming of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan with all power and false signs and wonders.
Revelation 13:13–14
It performs great signs, even making fire come down from heaven to earth in front of people, and by the signs that it is allowed to work... it deceives those who dwell on the earth.
The pattern Pharaoh's magicians turned staffs into serpents. They turned water to blood. They produced frogs (Ex 7–8). The signs were real. They just weren't from God. The test is never "did something miraculous happen?" — it's "what does the message say, and does it agree with what God has already revealed?"
ii.
Even angelic appearances must be tested

A radiant being claiming to bring a message from God is exactly the form Scripture warns about most explicitly. Paul's wording is striking: this is not a rare possibility but Satan's signature disguise.

2 Corinthians 11:13–15 — Paul
Such men are false apostles, deceitful workmen, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. And no wonder, for even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. So it is no surprise if his servants also disguise themselves.
Galatians 1:8 — Paul
Even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed.
Paul's astonishing claim Paul does not say "if a strange teacher comes." He says even if he himself, or an angel from heaven, brings a message that contradicts what was already delivered — reject it. The brightness of the messenger is not the test. The content is. A glowing visitor with a new revelation is not, in the Bible's account, automatically trustworthy. It may be exactly what we were told to be wary of.
iii.
What they say about Jesus

The New Testament makes this the decisive test. It is not whether a teacher mentions Jesus, or speaks of him with respect, or calls him a prophet. It is whether they confess him as he himself claimed to be — the eternal Son, the Lord (YHWH) come in flesh.

1 John 4:2–3
By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. This is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard was coming and now is in the world already.
1 John 2:22–23
Who is the liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the antichrist, he who denies the Father and the Son. No one who denies the Son has the Father.
John 8:58 — Jesus' own claim
Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I AM.
John 20:28 — Thomas to the risen Jesus
My Lord and my God!
Romans 10:9
If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.
The line in the sand Jesus repeatedly took to himself the divine name — "I AM" — the same name God revealed to Moses at the burning bush (Ex 3:14). He accepted worship. He forgave sins. The earliest Christians, all of them Jewish monotheists, called him Lord (the Greek word their own Bible used to translate YHWH). Any teaching that demotes Jesus from this confession — however reverent it sounds toward him otherwise — fails this test by the Bible's own standard. 1 John 4:2–3 does not leave room for a middle position.
iv.
How they handle Scripture

The first deception in the Bible was not an outright lie. It was a subtle re-quoting of God's words. The serpent didn't tell Eve there was no God — he asked her, "Did God really say?" The pattern continues throughout Scripture: deception almost always comes through the Bible, not against it. It uses the words and bends them.

Genesis 3:1, 4–5 — the serpent
Did God actually say, 'You shall not eat of any tree in the garden'?... You will not surely die. For God knows that when you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God.
Matthew 4:5–7 — Satan tempts Jesus by quoting Psalm 91
If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down, for it is written, 'He will command his angels concerning you'... Jesus said to him, 'Again it is written, You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.'
2 Peter 3:16
There are some things in [Paul's letters] that are hard to understand, which the ignorant and unstable twist to their own destruction, as they do the other Scriptures.
2 Corinthians 4:2 — Paul
We have renounced disgraceful, underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to tamper with God's word.
The serpent's method Notice what Satan does in Matthew 4 — he quotes Scripture. Accurately, even. The deception is not in inventing new verses but in using real ones in the wrong way. A teaching that selectively borrows from the Bible, retells its stories with crucial details altered, or claims to "correct" or "complete" what Scripture already records — these are exactly the patterns Genesis 3 was meant to warn us about.
v.
Borrowed authority & "another gospel"

Counterfeit currency works because it looks like real currency. False teachings rarely arrive announcing themselves as opposed to the Bible — they arrive draped in it, honoring it, claiming continuity with it, and then quietly setting it aside in favor of something newer.

Galatians 1:6–9 — Paul
I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting him who called you in the grace of Christ and are turning to a different gospel — not that there is another one, but there are some who trouble you and want to distort the gospel of Christ. But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed.
Revelation 22:18–19 — the closing words of the Bible
I warn everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: if anyone adds to them, God will add to him the plagues described in this book, and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God will take away his share in the tree of life.
Jude 3
Contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints.
Once for all Jude calls the faith something "once for all delivered" — not something requiring later supplements, corrections, or additional revelations. Paul says even an angel bringing a different gospel is to be rejected. A movement that honors Moses, honors Jesus to a point, and then introduces a new and final revelation that overrides what came before — Scripture treats this not as a fulfillment but as the very thing it warned about.
vi.
By their fruit you will know them

Jesus' practical test. Not "by their words" alone, not "by their signs," but by the long, observable shape of a life and a movement.

Matthew 7:16–20 — Jesus
You will recognize them by their fruits. Are grapes gathered from thornbushes, or figs from thistles? So, every healthy tree bears good fruit, but the diseased tree bears bad fruit... Thus you will recognize them by their fruits.
Galatians 5:22–23 — the fruit of the Spirit
The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law.
Matthew 26:52 — Jesus at his arrest
Put your sword back into its place. For all who take the sword will perish by the sword.
What kind of tree? A teaching can be evaluated by what it produces in its first generation, when its founder is still alive and shaping it directly. Was the founder's life marked by the fruit of the Spirit — love, gentleness, patience, self-control? Did his teaching spread by the willing testimony of those who suffered for it, or by political and military power compelling assent? This is Jesus' own test.
vii.
Sincere persecutors

One of the most sobering warnings in Scripture: those who harm the followers of Jesus may not be wicked schemers. They may be deeply religious people who genuinely believe they are serving God.

John 16:2 — Jesus to his disciples
They will put you out of the synagogues. Indeed, the hour is coming when whoever kills you will think he is offering service to God.
Acts 26:9–11 — Paul, recalling his life as a persecutor
I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things in opposing the name of Jesus of Nazareth. And I did so in Jerusalem. I not only locked up many of the saints in prison... but when they were put to death I cast my vote against them.
Revelation 6:9–10
I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain for the word of God and for the witness they had borne.
Revelation 20:4
I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and those who had not worshiped the beast or its image and had not received its mark on their foreheads or their hands. They came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years.
Paul's sobering self-knowledge Paul (then Saul) was not a casual man. He was one of the most learned, most zealous, most religiously sincere men of his generation. He hunted Christians believing he was defending God's honor — until he met the risen Jesus on the road to Damascus and discovered, to his horror, that the One he had been persecuting was the Lord. Sincerity is not the test. Zeal is not the test. Acts 26 tells us a person can be sincerely, religiously, devoutly wrong — and a teaching that produces such people, generation after generation, raises the question Scripture itself raises.
III · The Sum

A summary of the framework

Held together, Scripture's tests for any prophet, teacher, vision, or movement form a coherent picture. None of these tests is meant to be applied alone — together, they are how the Bible asks us to weigh anyone, including the people we already follow.

The summary
A messenger is to be tested — even an angel, even one who works miracles — by whether their message agrees with what God has already revealed; by whether they confess Jesus as the Lord come in flesh; by whether they handle Scripture honestly rather than twisting it; by whether their teaching adds to or alters the gospel "once for all delivered"; by the fruit of the founder's life and the movement's spread; and by whether their followers, even when sincere, find themselves opposing the very people Christ called his own. The Bible is not afraid of these tests. It invites them.
A Closing Blessing · The Berean Mind

"They examined the Scriptures every day."

When Paul came to Berea, the Jews there did something remarkable. They didn't reject his message — but they didn't simply accept it either. They went home and tested it.

Acts 17:11
Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so.

Luke calls them more noble for this. Not less faithful — more. The Bible never asks anyone to suspend their judgment in the name of belief. It asks the opposite. Receive the word with eagerness; then examine the Scriptures to see if it holds.

That's what these tests are for. Apply them honestly, to anyone — to the teachers of any tradition, including ones we already trust. A faith that cannot survive that examination was not worth holding; a faith that can, is worth everything.
